What a BESS record holds

The fields a real analysis needs

Not a headline count — the row-level detail you would otherwise assemble by hand from EIA spreadsheets and press releases.

▮

Power and energy

Nameplate power in MW and, where the operator reports it, energy capacity in MWh — the pair that tells you whether a site is a frequency-response asset or a four-hour shifting asset.

▣

Operator and ownership

The entity EIA has on file for the plant, normalized so a portfolio reads as one operator instead of six spellings.

◆

Status and year online

Operating, under construction, or planned, with a target year — so you can separate today's fleet from the pipeline.

◈

Location and market

City, state and coordinates, plus the ISO/RTO the site sits inside (ERCOT, CAISO, PJM, MISO, SPP, NYISO, ISO-NE) on the map.

Where the battery data comes from

Battery records are ingested from EIA-860M, the U.S. Energy Information Administration's monthly generator inventory, covering utility-scale plants of 1 MW or more that report to the federal government. The ingest runs after each monthly release; the change log records every project added, removed, or moved between statuses.

Because EIA-860M is a U.S. government work in the public domain, these records carry no upstream redistribution restriction — which is usually the sticking point when a third-party market layer goes into a customer-facing product. It is not one here. The methodology covers definitions, normalization, and what the federal inventory does not include (behind-the-meter and small commercial systems below the 1 MW reporting threshold).
